From 12% to 37% iOS Activation, Three Cycles, No New Feature

A driver takes thirty business trips this month and remembers maybe three.

The other twenty-seven were deductible mileage that nobody will claim. Driversnote captures those drives in the background and fills the logbook for them. Activation depends on getting users to that logbook sooner.

I led the 2022 redesign and both 2025 cycles.

iOS Permission Prompts, Rebuilt to Ask Cleanly

iOS auto-tracking activation sat at 12% in 2022, against a feature that did nothing until users granted location and motion.

I rebuilt the permission prompts so the asks landed cleanly. Friction lived in how the prompts asked, not in whether users were willing. 28-day activation moved from 12% to 37%, the largest single lift the team had seen on iOS.

The number held, and four years of cycles went elsewhere.

The Shortest Route to a Logbook That Logs Itself

Four years on, two cycles pulled the first logged drive closer to sign-up.

I led the onboarding reorder, where every other setup step got delayed so users met that drive sooner: captured in the background, mileage value already calculated, the logbook filling itself. Same quarter, I led Welcome Screens at the top of the funnel, where five visual variants ran and the static illustration won.

Both shipped within the quarter.

Welcome Screens, Onboarding, Permissions: Three Cycles, Three Activation Lifts

The 2025 numbers came back this spring.

The onboarding reorder concluded at 4% iOS and 8% Android against a 5% target, English markets only. Welcome Screens lifted 7-day activation by 2 to 3%, Android clearly significant, iOS suspended for translations. Three cycles in four years, each one pulling more users to a logbook the app filled for them.

No new feature in any of them.
